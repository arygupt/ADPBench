## GLM: actionable rejection captured

The saved, credential-redacted error has `param: "thinking"`, type
`invalid_request_error`, and the upstream message `json: unknown field "thinking"`.
The request included `thinking: {type: enabled}` and `reasoning_effort: low`, as
in the old batch, but this diagnostic used streaming, a small prompt and a
128-token cap. The current rejection explicitly names **thinking**, not low
reasoning effort or an output limit.

This is strong evidence for the same payload incompatibility in the old GLM
failure, but its discarded response body cannot be recovered and the exact
historical cause is not proven. Keep that original failure unchanged.

Next: remove `thinking` from a new reviewed GLM request profile and validate that
single change in a separately authorized tiny request. That correction and
follow-up request have **not** been performed by this six-request diagnostic.

Subsequent update: the [separately authorized follow-up](go-canary-followup-20260923.md)
removed that field and completed both GLM and MiniMax answers. This original
run's failure and receipt remain unchanged.

## Limits of this test

- MiniMax's stream and accounting worked, but all 128 output tokens were reported
  as thinking. It did not demonstrate a completed answer. A short diagnostic cap
  is not a finding that its full benchmark limit will also be exhausted.
- These short requests do not test provider-maximum output-limit acceptance,
  benchmark RTL correctness, long-request stability or scoring performance.
- The failed Actions conclusion remains visible because GLM was rejected. This
  diagnostic is deliberately excluded from benchmark results publication.

The [machine-readable receipt](go-canary-20260923.json) records source identity,
downloaded evidence hashes, outcomes and known/unknown usage. Private raw
responses and the bounded GLM error body remain in the Actions artifact
`go-canary-35816113463`. No raw reasoning or credentials are published here.
